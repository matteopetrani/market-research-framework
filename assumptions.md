# Nine Moons - Product Assumptions

This document contains key hypotheses about the Nine Moons pregnancy app concept. These assumptions will be tested and validated through research.

## Market Assumptions

### 1. Market Demand
**Assumption:** Pregnant women interested in spirituality represent a cohesive, addressable market segment. In particular, they are interested in getting inspired by beliefs and traditional stories about pregnancy (week by week) from different cultures, religions and beliefs. 

**Why this matters:** Validates the core target audience exists and is identifiable.

**Validation criteria:**
- Evidence of spiritual/holistic interest in pregnancy communities
- Size of the addressable market segment
- User behavior patterns indicating this is a distinct cohort

---

### 2. Market Size
**Assumption:** The spiritual pregnancy segment is large enough to support a viable business (>50K yearly potential users).

**Why this matters:** Determines if the market is worth pursuing.

**Validation criteria:**
- TAM/SAM/SOM calculations
- Growth trends in wellness + pregnancy categories
- Competitor user bases in adjacent segments

---

## Competitive Assumptions

### 3. Competitive Gaps
**Assumption:** Existing pregnancy apps lack comprehensive spiritual and religious content. The editorial content provided by comeptitor is just science based (size of the fetus, weight, evolution, ...)

**Why this matters:** Validates the key differentiation opportunity.

**Validation criteria:**
- Competitor feature analysis shows limited spiritual content
- User complaints about missing spiritual perspectives
- No clear market leader in this niche

---

### 4. Spiritual Diversity
**Assumption:** Users want content spanning multiple spiritual traditions, not just one religious perspective.

**Why this matters:** Informs content strategy (broad vs. focused).

**Validation criteria:**
- User discussions mention diverse spiritual interests
- Competitors focusing on single traditions show demand for alternatives
- Evidence of multi-faith pregnancy communities

---

## User Assumptions

### 5. Pain Points
**Assumption:** Pregnant users feel existing apps are too clinical/medical and lack emotional/spiritual support.

**Why this matters:** Validates the problem we're solving.

**Validation criteria:**
- User complaints in reviews and communities
- Sentiment analysis shows desire for holistic approaches
- Feature requests for spiritual/emotional content

---

### 6. Content Preferences
**Assumption:** Users want weekly spiritual content (reflections, rituals, meditations) integrated with pregnancy tracking.

**Why this matters:** Defines the core product experience.

**Validation criteria:**
- User requests for weekly content in communities
- Success of meditation/wellness apps with weekly formats
- Engagement patterns with spiritual pregnancy content

---

## Monetization Assumptions

### 7. Willingness to Pay
**Assumption:** Users will pay a subscription fee ($1.5-2/wek) for premium spiritual pregnancy content.

**Why this matters:** Validates the business model.

**Validation criteria:**
- Competitor pricing analysis
- Subscription success in pregnancy + wellness categories
- User discussions about app spending

---

### 8. Freemium Viability
**Assumption:** A freemium model (3 weeks free then subscription) will drive conversion better than free trial or paid-only.

**Why this matters:** Determines launch monetization strategy.

**Validation criteria:**
- Competitor monetization model analysis
- Industry benchmarks for freemium pregnancy apps
- User sentiment about paywalls

---

## Product Assumptions

### 9a. Content Over Features
**Assumption:** High-quality spiritual content is more important than advanced tracking features for our target users. The app is more a container of an editorial project.

**Why this matters:** Prioritizes MVP scope.

**Validation criteria:**
- User feedback emphasizes content quality
- Competitors with basic tracking + good content succeed
- Feature request patterns show content gaps more than feature gaps

---

### 9b. Content existence 
**Assumption:** There is enough content to write relevant weekly updates during the maternity. Each post/story must be tied and contextualized with the current week of the fetus growth. There should not be generalistic content such as "in the third month..." but "during the 13th week the ancient populus of... used to...". Content should be inspiring, surprising and nurture curiosity. 

**Why this matters:** Define the content strategy. 

**Validation criteria:**
- There is enough content to cover the whole pregnancy time span

---

### 10. Cross-Platform Necessity
**Assumption:** We need both iOS and Android at launch to reach critical mass.

**Why this matters:** Determines development complexity and timeline.

**Validation criteria:**
- Platform distribution of target users
- Competitor platform strategies
- User platform preferences in pregnancy communities

---

## Distribution Assumptions

### 11. Organic Discovery
**Assumption:** Users will discover Nine Moons through App Store search, social media, and pregnancy communities.

**Why this matters:** Validates go-to-market approach.

**Validation criteria:**
- How users currently discover pregnancy apps
- Competitor acquisition channels
- Community receptiveness to new app recommendations

---

### 12. Community Building
**Assumption:** Building a community around spiritual pregnancy will drive retention and word-of-mouth growth.

**Why this matters:** Informs long-term engagement strategy.

**Validation criteria:**
- Success of community features in competitor apps
- Engagement levels in spiritual pregnancy communities
- User desire for connection with like-minded pregnant people

---
